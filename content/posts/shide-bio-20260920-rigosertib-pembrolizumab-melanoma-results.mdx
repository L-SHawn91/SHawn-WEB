The record does not show that the combination improved outcomes or that the listed adverse events were caused by treatment , and  the result is difficult to interpret because the study had one treatment group and was terminated when the drug became unavailable .

## The posted results show no recorded response in the treated cohort

The registry describes a phase II interventional trial for adults whose metastatic melanoma had not responded to earlier PD-1 inhibitor treatment. Everyone received oral rigosertib with intravenous pembrolizumab in the same treatment group. The efficacy table used seven participants as its denominator and recorded an overall response value of 0.  Overall response rate (ORR) counts complete or partial tumor responses under the registry’s iRECIST definition.  That is a result from this small posted cohort, not a statement about every patient with melanoma or every use of the two medicines.

The design matters because it answers a narrower question than a randomized comparison would. The registry lists one treatment group, no masking, and no parallel comparator. The planned assessment followed tumor imaging every eight weeks through Week 24 and then every twelve weeks until progression, for up to two years. The analysis therefore tells us what happened among the participants entered into this combination study; it cannot show whether the regimen performed better than another treatment, supportive care, or the expected course after prior PD-1 therapy.

**Design:** Phase II, single-group, unmasked interventional trial.

**Enrolled:** 7 actual participants with refractory metastatic melanoma.

**Efficacy analysis:** 7 participants.

**Primary endpoint:** Overall response rate by iRECIST 1.1.

## The survival entries add context but do not turn the record into a treatment comparison

The registry posted a median progression-free survival of 1.88 months. The lower 95% confidence limit was 1.84 months, while the upper limit was not reached at the data cutoff. A separate one-year progression-free survival entry was 14%, with a 95% confidence interval from 2.3% to 88%.  Progression-free survival measures the time until documented progression or death, using the study’s prespecified assessment method.  In a cohort this small, the wide interval is not a footnote; it is part of what the number means.

The registry also lists a median overall survival of 6.97 months, with a lower 95% confidence limit of 2.5 months and an upper limit not reached at the cutoff. These entries describe the participants in this record. They do not establish that the combination caused the observed survival time, because there was no control group and the analysis is vulnerable to the small number of participants and follow-up events.

The clearest reading is therefore limited. In this posted group of patients whose disease had already resisted PD-1 inhibition, the registry did not record an objective response, and the time-to-event entries were short or imprecise. That information may matter when clinicians and researchers compare the result with other evidence, but it does not support a claim that the regimen is broadly ineffective or that a different treatment is appropriate for an individual patient.

## Termination and the safety table limit what can be attributed to treatment

The registry status is TERMINATED, with drug unavailable listed as the reason. That is a logistical reason for stopping the trial, not a finding that the regimen was stopped because of efficacy or toxicity. The adverse-event module says events were collected during treatment and through 30 days after the last dose, for up to two years. Its event-group table lists 4 participants affected by death among 7 at risk, 7 participants with serious adverse events among 7 at risk, and 7 with other adverse events among 7 at risk. Those fields describe what the registry recorded; they do not identify the cause of death or prove that treatment produced any listed event.

The source also has a separate treatment-related toxicity outcome classified with CTCAE version 5.0. Keeping those layers separate matters. An adverse-event count, a treatment-related toxicity entry, and a clinical judgment about causality are not interchangeable. The posted record does not provide a controlled comparison that would let readers subtract background risk, disease progression, or other care from the observed counts.

Four deaths in a seven-person risk set make the safety boundary especially important. The number should be visible rather than hidden, but it should not be converted into a treatment-causality claim. The registry record is a structured results source, not a peer-reviewed adjudication of why each participant experienced an event. Any medical decision would require the complete clinical record and qualified professional review.

## The next interpretation depends on fuller evidence, not a stronger headline

For this result to support a firmer clinical conclusion, readers would need a clearer accounting of participant flow, the timing of progression and death, treatment exposure, missing assessments, and the clinical attribution of adverse events. A larger cohort or a comparison group would also be needed to separate the combination’s possible contribution from the difficult disease setting in which it was tested.

The registry entry can still be useful. It makes the actual denominator and the posted endpoint values visible, including the difference between seven people enrolled and seven people in the efficacy analysis. It also shows why a response number should be read beside the design, status, and safety fields. A result posted in a registry is evidence that an entry was made and reported; it is not the same as a peer-reviewed efficacy conclusion or a regulatory approval.

The practical takeaway is narrow: the September 16 results record reports no objective response in this seven-person, single-group melanoma cohort, alongside short and uncertain time-to-event estimates and serious safety limits. That is enough to describe what was posted. It is not enough to select treatment, predict an individual’s outcome, or erase the uncertainty around the recorded deaths.

This article separates the registry’s reported values from interpretation. The official record supports the study design, denominators, response and survival entries, termination reason, and adverse-event counts. It does not by itself establish comparative benefit, treatment causality, or how the result applies to a person outside this small refractory-melanoma cohort.
