## Snapshot: Stability After the Storm

The US pre-market is showing **stabilization signals** after Monday’s semiconductor decline. A cooler-than-expected CPI report is providing macro relief, while oil prices remain the main pressure point.

## Market Pulse

**Futures:** Nasdaq futures are leading with +1.08%, suggesting tech is poised for a recovery open. S&P 500 futures are slightly positive at +0.25%. The broad picture is stabilization, not acceleration in either direction.

**Semiconductors:** The sector is flat to slightly mixed in pre-market trading. After Monday’s sharp -4% to -5% decline across semiconductor ETFs, the lack of further deterioration is itself a positive signal. Individual names are mixed — some showing modest gains, others still adjusting — but no cascading downward pressure is evident.

**Macro Background:** The fear gauge (volatility index) is down -2.4%, Treasury yields are easing, and the US dollar is softening. This combination typically supports risk appetite and provides a favorable backdrop for equity markets.

**Oil Watch:** Crude oil remains elevated near $80.70, reflecting ongoing geopolitical tensions. While this is a headwind, Asian markets have already shown some ability to decouple from oil-driven panic.

## Korea Market Context

South Korean markets staged an impressive recovery on Tuesday, closing with a +5.5% surge from the midday low. The KOSPI finished at 6,856.83, with semiconductor leaders Samsung Electronics (+3.3%) and SK Hynix (+3.7%) leading the rebound. This “crash exhaustion” signal suggests the worst of the downward pressure has passed for now.

The Korean won continues to strengthen against the dollar, and institutional inflows provided the backbone for Tuesday’s recovery. These are positive signals for the Wednesday session, though the picture will depend on how US markets close tonight.

## What to Watch

– **First-hour US trading (23:40 KST):** Watch whether pre-market stability holds or fades. A constructive first hour in semiconductors would support further recovery in Korean markets on Wednesday.

– **Oil price ceiling:** WTI crude breaking above $82 could re-introduce pressure on manufacturing and export-heavy markets.

– **Individual semiconductor divergence:** NVDA weakness versus AMD/MU strength is a mixed signal — not yet a sector-wide concern but worth monitoring.

## Data Limitations

Pre-market price data reflects low-liquidity extended-hours trading and may not represent regular-session opening prices. Futures data is more reliable as a directional indicator. This note is based on publicly available market data as of 22:20 KST.
