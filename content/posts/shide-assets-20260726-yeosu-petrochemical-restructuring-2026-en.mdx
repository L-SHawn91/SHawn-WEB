Korea’s Ministry of Trade, Industry and Resources (MOTIR) approved the business restructuring plans of four companies — Yeochun NCC, Lotte Chemical, Hanwha Solutions, and DL Chemical. This is the **second petrochemical industry restructuring project**, following the Daesan No. 1 project. This article explains who is merging what, where the money comes from, and what conditions attach to government support.

## 1) The Bottom Line: Approval Is a Starting Point, Not a Finish Line

According to the [FSC press release](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/87393) (2026-07-22), MOTIR approved the final restructuring plans of the four companies on July 20. Approval marks the **beginning of implementation**. Actual incorporation, asset transfers, facility integration, and workforce redeployment will proceed in subsequent phases.

> Business restructuring approval is granted under the Special Act on Corporate Vitalization (Corporate Vitalization Act). Approved companies receive tax, financial, and regulatory benefits, but plan compliance is subject to separate monitoring.

## 5) Why Petrochemical Restructuring Is Necessary

Korea’s petrochemical industry faces **oversupply and deteriorating profitability** driven by capacity expansion in China and the Middle East.

- NCC (Naphtha Cracking Center) : Produces basic olefins (ethylene, propylene). Weakening export competitiveness as China’s self-sufficiency rises.

- Downstream : Commodity resins (PE, PP, PVC). Intensifying price competition with low-cost Chinese and Middle Eastern products.

- High-value materials : Specialty and engineering plastics. Conversion investment required.

The core logic of restructuring is to integrate redundant facilities, raise utilization rates, and reinvest saved resources into high-value materials conversion.

## 8) Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. Does restructuring approval equal government grant disbursement?**

A. No. Approval grants eligibility for Corporate Vitalization Act benefits. Actual financial and tax support requires separate application and review procedures.

**Q. What is the impact on Lotte Chemical shareholders from the physical spin-off?**

A. A physical spin-off creates a separate entity with the parent holding 100% of its shares. There is no direct equity change for Lotte Chemical shareholders, but indirect effects may arise depending on the valuation of spun-off assets.

**Q. Is Yeochun NCC a listed company?**

A. Yeochun NCC is an unlisted corporation. Hanwha Solutions and DL Chemical each hold 50% equity.

**Q. Do Daesan No. 1 and Yeosu No. 2 support overlap?**

A. Support is provided on a per-project basis, not per company. Lotte Chemical participates in both Daesan No. 1 and Yeosu No. 2, but support for each project is separate.

**Q. Will there be production disruptions during facility integration?**

A. The specific integration timeline and shutdown periods have not been disclosed. NCC integration typically proceeds through phased shutdown and restart.

## 9) Industrial Significance of Petrochemical Restructuring

Korea’s petrochemical industry grew around three major complexes — Yeosu, Daesan, and Ulsan — since the 1970s. However, since the 2020s, large-scale capacity expansion in China and the Middle East has weakened commodity product price competitiveness, and domestic NCC utilization rates have trended downward.

The core objectives of restructuring are:

- Economies of scale : Unit cost reduction through redundant facility integration

- Utilization recovery : Profitability improvement through optimized operation of integrated facilities

- High-value conversion : Reinvestment of saved resources into specialty and engineering plastics

- Carbon neutrality response : Energy efficiency improvement through aging facility integration

Yeosu No. 2, together with Daesan No. 1, is expected to serve as a litmus test for Korea’s petrochemical industry structural transformation.
