## US First Hour — Rotation, Not Panic

US markets opened with semiconductor gap-ups, but heavy profit-taking emerged within the first hour. SOXX -3.25%, AMD -5.29%, MU -7.24%. Yet the S&P 500 (+0.19%), Dow (+0.38%), Russell 2000 (+0.55%), and financials (+1.09%) all rose. The VIX dropped to 16.18 — this is an **orderly sector rotation** after two days of massive chip gains, not a systemic selloff.

### Key Signals

- 🔴 SOXX (Chip ETF): -3.25% — opened +1.4%, heavy fade

- 🔴 AMD: -5.29% — opened +2%, worst reversal among large chips

- 🔴 MU (Micron): -7.24% — memory chip hit hardest

- 🟢 S&P 500 (SPY): +0.19% — broad market holding up

- 🟢 Dow (DIA): +0.38% — value rotation in progress

- 🟢 Russell 2000 (IWM): +0.55% — small-caps rising

- 🟢 Financials (XLF): +1.09% — strongest sector

- 🟢 VIX: 16.18 (down) — fear gauge declining, no panic

- 🔴 EWY (Korea ETF): -4.12% — concentrated semiconductor weight

### What This Means

The sharp semiconductor selloff is **concentrated profit-taking** after two days of outsized gains. The broader market continues to rise and the VIX is declining — confirming rotation, not panic. Near-term pressure on chip names is likely, but constrained by the broader market’s resilience.
