Korea has announced a KRW 150 billion “K-Culture Value-Up Fund.” The headline number can sound as if a large pool of capital has already been raised and released. It has not. **The authorities have opened the process for selecting a fund manager, with KRW 150 billion as the target fund size.** The private-capital raise, fund closing and investments in individual companies still lie ahead. This explainer separates what has been fixed from what remains undecided.

## The short answer: KRW 150 billion is a target, not money already deployed

The [Financial Services Commission](https://www.fsc.go.kr/no010101/87388) and the [Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism](https://www.mcst.go.kr/site/s_notice/press/pressView.jsp?pSeq=22598) said the manager-selection process opened on July 22, 2026. The target fund size is **KRW 150 billion**.

The announced formation plan consists of:

- KRW 50 billion in MCST fiscal capital

- KRW 30 billion from the Korea Development Bank and the High-Tech Strategic Industry Fund

- At least KRW 70 billion in private capital to be raised

The precise description is therefore that Korea has started selecting a manager for a fund targeting KRW 150 billion. It is too early to say that the fund has closed or that KRW 150 billion has been distributed to companies.

## Where the target capital is meant to go

The target fund is divided into two investment tracks.

- AI and IP: KRW 100 billion This track is intended for companies that use AI in content planning or production, and for K-culture businesses that create value from content IP in areas such as games, video and music.

- Content Innovation: KRW 50 billion This track is designed for content-industry companies engaged in production, distribution and related economic activity.

“AI” does not mean a stand-alone technology sector here. The official definition covers AI or AI-based technology that can create synergies with K-culture. “IP utilization” likewise means generating value from owned rights or from business rights such as licenses and usage rights, rather than simply holding a recognizable property.

## The 50%, 25% and 25% figures are obligations, not pie slices

The official table sets “primary-purpose investment ratios” for each track.

### KRW 100 billion AI and IP track

- Domestic companies operating in K-culture industries: 50% of the target fund

- Content companies applying AI-based technology: 25%

- K-culture companies utilizing content IP: 25%

- The AI-content and IP-utilization categories cannot be double-counted against each other

### KRW 50 billion Content Innovation track

- Domestic companies operating in K-culture industries: 50% of the target fund

- Content-innovation companies engaged in activities such as production and distribution: 15%

These numbers should not be added mechanically and drawn as slices of one pie. They are **investment obligations or floors**. The detailed fund rules will determine how overarching and specific categories interact. The one overlap rule stated explicitly in the release is that the AI-content 25% and IP-utilization 25% categories cannot be double-counted.

## What is different from earlier policy funds

According to the ministries, earlier culture and content policy funds were generally around **KRW 50 billion**, limiting their ability to make large investments in expensive productions. The new structure connects MCST policy finance with capital from KDB and the High-Tech Strategic Industry Fund, raising the overall target to KRW 150 billion and creating room for larger individual deals.

The important change is not merely “more culture spending.” The government describes this as the first case in which the National Growth Fund has joined a ministry’s policy fund, while the participation of the High-Tech Strategic Industry Fund places content within a broader advanced-industry financing framework.

“Room for larger deals,” however, is an institutional possibility. The releases do not disclose the amount, terms or timing of any company-level investment.

## Which companies could receive investment

No company or genre has been selected in the published material. The eligible universe is broad and includes:

- content industries and related sectors defined under Korea’s Content Industry Promotion Act

- Korean Wave and Korean Wave-related industries defined under the relevant framework act

- domestic companies combining AI with content planning, production or distribution

- domestic businesses creating value from content IP in games, video, music and related fields

- content-industry companies engaged in production, distribution and related commercial activity

This is an **eligible investment universe**, not an automatic beneficiary list. A manager must first be selected, the fund must be formed, and potential investments must pass the manager’s review and contracting process. The current manager-selection notice is not a direct grant or application notice for ordinary content companies.

## Timeline: manager proposals in August, selection planned for September

The official schedule is:

- July 22, 2026: manager-selection notice opened

- August 12, 2026, 10:00–16:00 KST: manager proposals accepted

- September 2026: final manager selection planned

- After that: private fundraising, fund closing and individual investments; the releases give no fixed dates

The August 12 submission window is for **prospective fund managers**, not for content companies seeking capital. Detailed scheduling is to be provided through Korea Growth Investment Corporation, the fiscal master-fund manager.

## Five things to watch next

The fund’s significance will be determined less by the headline target than by what happens after manager selection.

- Whether at least KRW 70 billion in private capital is actually raised The final close may differ from the target.

- The selected manager’s expertise Content production, AI technology and IP commercialization require different underwriting and exit capabilities.

- Company-level ticket sizes and follow-on rules The ability to make large investments does not mean large deals have already been approved.

- How genuine value creation in AI and IP is assessed Using an AI tool or owning a known IP does not by itself establish a durable business advantage.

- How policy outcomes and financial performance are measured separately Export revenue, IP expansion, production capability and employment are industry outcomes; investment recovery is a different measure.

## Final assessment: the rules after closing matter more than the headline size

The K-Culture Value-Up Fund targets a scale larger than the roughly KRW 50 billion policy funds cited by the government. Its design allocates KRW 100 billion to AI and IP and KRW 50 billion to Content Innovation, linking culture finance to a broader advanced-industry capital pool.

But the project remains at the **manager-selection stage**. The KRW 70 billion-plus private component is a fundraising target. The final close, manager, company-level terms and portfolio have not been announced.

> Korea has not already invested KRW 150 billion in K-culture. It has started the process of selecting a manager and forming a fund with KRW 150 billion as the target.
