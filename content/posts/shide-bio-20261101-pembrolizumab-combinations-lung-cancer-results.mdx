## Registry results show responses in both treatment arms

ClinicalTrials.gov lists results for a Phase 2 study combining pembrolizumab with MK-4830 or MK-0482. The participant-flow table records 60 randomized and treated participants in the first group, and 40 assigned to the second, of whom 39 received treatment. Although the registry describes assignment under a larger master protocol, this substudy is labeled non-randomized; the two combinations were not tested in a randomized head-to-head comparison. These different denominators matter when reading the results.

**Study design:** open-label, non-randomized, parallel Phase 2 trial.

**Enrolled population:** 100 people with treatment-naive, advanced PD-L1-positive non-small cell lung cancer.

**Analysis population:** the registry reports response outcomes for 60 participants in the MK-4830 arm and 39 treated participants in the MK-0482 arm.

**Primary endpoint:** confirmed objective response rate (ORR), assessed by investigators using RECIST 1.1.  ORR is the share of participants with a confirmed complete or partial tumor response.

The registry reports an ORR of 25% among 60 participants in the MK-4830 combination arm (95% CI, 14.7–37.9) and 35.9% among 39 treated participants in the MK-0482 arm (95% CI, 21.2–52.8). ORR counts participants whose tumor measurements showed a confirmed complete or partial response under the study’s criteria. These percentages are registry-reported within-arm results, not evidence of a difference between regimens.

Median progression-free survival was 4.8 months (95% CI, 3.0 to 8.3) in the MK-4830 arm and 4.9 months (95% CI, 3.7 to 11.3) in the MK-0482 arm. This measure tracks time from first dose until documented progression or death. The registry reports no head-to-head test, so the similar medians do not establish equivalence.

The word “arm” here means a group assigned to one combination within this study, not a randomized comparison group. Because the registry identifies the allocation as non-randomized, the groups may differ in ways that affect their outcomes. The figures describe what happened in each group; they cannot isolate the effect of adding either investigational drug to pembrolizumab.

## The registry makes the response denominators visible

A response rate can look persuasive when shown alone. Here, the denominators and study design change how much can be concluded. The 25% and 35.9% figures refer to separate groups receiving different combinations, with different numbers treated. The registry does not report a randomized comparison or a statistical test establishing that the observed difference reflects the drugs rather than differences between participants or chance.

That distinction matters for people following treatment research. A result posted in a trial registry is a direct record of the sponsor’s submitted outcomes, but it is not the same as a peer-reviewed paper that presents the full analysis and its context. It also does not mean that either combination has been approved for clinical use. The record identifies the trial as completed; approval status is a separate regulatory decision.

The outcome definitions help explain what these numbers do and do not capture. A confirmed response requires a qualifying change in measured tumors. It does not by itself tell us how long a response lasted or whether people felt better. The registry also reports median progression-free survival, but a median does not describe every participant’s experience. Some people may have progressed earlier and others later.

Read together, the response and progression-free survival measures offer a limited snapshot of activity in these study groups. They do not establish a treatment recommendation. The result may help researchers decide what deserves closer study, while a larger controlled trial would be needed to test whether one regimen adds benefit over another approach.

## Safety counts need the same care as efficacy results

The registry’s all-cause mortality table lists 40 deaths among 60 participants at risk in the MK-4830 group, and 23 among 40 at risk in the MK-0482 group. These are deaths recorded during the study, not counts attributed to study treatment. They must not be compared as evidence that one combination was safer: the arms were not randomized, their participants and follow-up may differ, and these totals do not identify cause.

Serious adverse events were reported for 30 of 60 participants in the MK-4830 group and 13 of 39 treated participants in the MK-0482 group. The registry says serious and other adverse events were collected among participants who received at least one dose, and its adverse-event definition includes events whether or not they were considered caused by treatment. The posted summary therefore cannot turn each event into a treatment effect.

The registry lists diarrhea, fatigue, fever, and pruritus among the recorded other adverse events. In the MK-4830 group, diarrhea, fatigue, fever, and pruritus affected 11 of 60 participants each. In the MK-0482 group, fatigue affected 10 of 39, pruritus 9, diarrhea 7, and fever 4. These are all-cause registry entries, not necessarily treatment-related rates. The public record does not provide a comparative safety test.

The trial was open-label, and outcome assessments were made by investigators. The registry describes ORR as confirmed responses according to RECIST 1.1, but this remains an investigator-assessed measure. Approximate outcome windows extend to about 56 months for response and progression-free survival; this is not the same as saying every participant was followed for that length of time.

## A controlled comparison would answer the question left open

The next useful evidence would show whether either combination improves outcomes when compared under a design that can separate treatment effects from differences in the people enrolled. That assessment would need prespecified endpoints, clear analysis populations, longer-term outcome reporting, and safety results with consistent definitions. The current registry posting does not provide that comparison.

For now, the record supports a narrower conclusion: confirmed tumor responses were reported in both combination arms, and the registry also reports progression-free survival and substantial adverse-event counts. The percentages cannot identify a preferred treatment, and the all-cause death figures cannot be assigned to the study drugs from this record.

ClinicalTrials.gov first posted the results on 2026-10-29 and lists the study status as completed. The results are useful because the denominators and safety tables are now accessible, but they remain registry results rather than a peer-reviewed account or a regulatory decision. Anyone making care decisions should rely on their oncology team, not on these group-level figures.

This article summarizes the ClinicalTrials.gov record. It reports the registry’s within-arm results and does not infer a causal or head-to-head treatment effect.
