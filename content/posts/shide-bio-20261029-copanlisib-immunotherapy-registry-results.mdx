## The registry reports a dose-limiting toxicity in one small group

The registry separates the study into four groups rather than one pooled treatment population. Three expansion groups in Trial 1 received copanlisib with nivolumab: 16 people with PIK3CA mutations, 18 with PTEN mutations, and 16 whose tumors were wild type for both genes. A separate dose-escalation group in Trial 2, with 2 people, received copanlisib, nivolumab, and ipilimumab. Although the protocol lists actual enrollment of 54, the results tables show 52 people across these four groups. Those figures describe different parts of the record and should not be merged or treated as interchangeable.

The primary outcome was dose-limiting toxicity during the first and second 28-day treatment cycles, a short early window used to assess whether a dose combination can be taken forward. The registry lists no such events in the three expansion groups among evaluable participants. In the two-person dose-escalation group, one participant was categorized with a dose-limiting toxicity and one was not evaluable. The registry does not describe the specific symptoms or clinical details of that DLT. The small group and different regimen cannot be compared with the expansion cohorts as if they were randomized arms.

The expansion groups also had participants who could not be evaluated for this endpoint: one in the PIK3CA group, three in the PTEN group, and one in the wild-type group. The registry therefore distinguishes the people assessed for dose-limiting toxicity from the people listed in each safety group. A zero in the DLT category does not mean that no adverse events occurred, and it does not describe later tumor response. The posted result is about a defined safety endpoint, not an overall judgment that the regimen was safe or effective.

## The posted results answer a dose question, not whether tumors shrank

The National Cancer Institute-sponsored trial tested copanlisib combinations in advanced solid tumors grouped by molecular features. That study rationale does not establish that the regimens improve outcomes for patients.

The registry lists objective response, clinical benefit, progression-free survival, and overall survival as secondary outcomes, but the registry record provides no results for them. In particular, it contains no  objective response rate, the share of participants whose tumors met the trial’s complete or partial response criteria . Missing results do not show that treatment failed, but this update cannot establish tumor control or the size and duration of any benefit.

An early dose-finding trial asks a narrower question than a comparative treatment study. The registry reports separate molecular-profile and trial-part groups, not randomized arms. These results therefore cannot estimate whether adding copanlisib changed response or survival. The single dose-limiting toxicity in the two-person triple-combination group cannot be generalized as a reliable rate.

The registry lists a clinical benefit rate that includes stable disease lasting beyond six months, but gives no result for it. A listed endpoint is a planned measure, not a finding. A later registry update or peer-reviewed report would be needed to assess tumor control.

## Serious events and deaths appear in the registry, without proof of cause

The registry reports serious adverse events in 9/16, 7/18, 6/16, and 2/2 people across its four groups. The separate-group totals do not show whether treatment caused an individual event.

Deaths are listed in 4/16, 4/18, 5/16, and 0/2 people in those same groups. The record does not establish when or why the deaths occurred or whether treatment caused them.

Adverse events were collected from consent through up to two years after the last dose for treatment-related events. The registry reports only each patient’s highest grade, so these summaries do not show each person’s full course.

The specific serious-event entries include hyperglycemia in 3/16 people in the PIK3CA group; suspected pneumonitis and respiratory failure, one of 16 each; and Stevens-Johnson syndrome and colitis, one of 16 each in the wild-type group. Death NOS and COVID-19 each appear in one of 18 people in the PTEN group. Disease progression appears in 2/16 PIK3CA and one of 16 wild-type participants. ALT and AST increases each appear in 2/16 wild-type participants and one of two in the dose-escalation group. These registry entries do not establish treatment causation.

Nausea was reported in 9/16, 7/18, 8/16, and 1/2 people across the four groups. Anorexia, hypertension, and diarrhea also appear. The cohorts were small and differed in molecular profile and regimen, so the counts cannot establish which treatment is safer or whether a particular event was caused by treatment.

## Efficacy results and the full study report remain the next evidence to check

The registry currently labels the trial active but not recruiting, with its status verified in October 2026. The results were first posted on October 27, 2026. That publication date makes the registry entry timely, but a newly posted record is not the same as a peer-reviewed paper, regulatory decision, or practice guideline.

The most useful next update would report the planned response and survival outcomes with their analysis populations and denominators, alongside the safety information. A full report could clarify how the protocol enrollment relates to the 52 people represented in the displayed results groups, and describe the status of participants not evaluable for the primary endpoint. The current record does not resolve those questions, so they should stay open rather than be filled by calculation.

For now, the defensible takeaway is modest: the registry has added early dose-finding and safety observations for several molecularly defined groups, including one dose-limiting toxicity in a two-person three-drug group. It has not posted the listed tumor-response or survival results. Anyone considering treatment should rely on their oncology team and the complete evidence available for their situation; this registry summary cannot determine which treatment is appropriate.

This article describes the posted ClinicalTrials.gov record. Registry results are not a peer-reviewed publication, and the safety counts do not establish that treatment caused an event. No results for the listed secondary efficacy outcomes appear in the record.
