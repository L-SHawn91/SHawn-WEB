## Registry results show longer reported EFS medians and more complete responses

The registry reports higher pathological response rates and longer EFS medians, but its primary EFS comparison did not meet the conventional significance threshold.

In the primary Stage IIB–IIIB wild-type population assessed by an independent review facility, median event-free survival was 34.92 months in the placebo-plus-chemotherapy group (95% CI, 24.57–63.80; n=201) and 62.78 months with atezolizumab plus chemotherapy (95% CI, 41.43 to an upper bound that could not be estimated; n=196). The reported hazard ratio was 0.77 (95% CI, 0.58–1.02; p=0.0704). Because the interval includes 1 and the p-value is above 0.05, this primary comparison was not statistically significant at the conventional threshold. The longer median in the atezolizumab group is an observed result, not proof of a survival benefit.

In wild-type ITT (n=221 control; 219 atezolizumab), median EFS was 43.86 months (95% CI, 28.02–63.80) and 67.15 months (49.15 to not estimable). HR was 0.75 (0.57–0.98); no p-value was posted. Both EFS measures are primary outcomes.

At surgery, pCR was reported in 8.5% of controls (95% CI, 5.00–13.20; n=201) and 29.6% with atezolizumab (95% CI, 23.30–36.51; n=196). The measure means no viable tumor cells were found in the primary tumor and sampled nodes. Participants without surgery counted as non-responders.

The EGFR/ALK wild-type ITT pCR rates were 8.6% (95% CI, 5.26–13.10; n=221) and 30.6% (24.56–37.16; n=219). pCR is secondary; between-group differences were 21.13 percentage points (95% CI, 13.68–28.59) in the Stage IIB–IIIB wild-type population and 22.00 points (14.86–29.13) in wild-type ITT.

## The treatment sequence, not one isolated drug dose, was tested

For patients with resectable lung cancer, tumor shrinkage before surgery is only one part of the treatment question. The team also needs to know whether the approach changes recurrence, whether surgery remains feasible, how much toxicity it adds, and whether any difference persists after treatment. The trial tested an entire treatment sequence: four pre-operative cycles, surgery, then up to 16 post-operative cycles of atezolizumab in one group. This design cannot separate the contribution of the pre-operative drug from that of the later treatment.

 Event-free survival measures time until progression prevents surgery, cancer recurs, a new primary lung cancer appears, or death occurs.  The primary comparison’s HR was 0.77, with a 95% CI of 0.58–1.02 and p=0.0704; the interval crossed 1, so this comparison did not meet the conventional significance threshold. In the separately reported EGFR/ALK wild-type ITT population, the HR was 0.75 (95% CI, 0.57–0.98), with no p-value posted. Both EFS measures are labeled primary in the registry; pCR is secondary. These estimates describe different populations and should not be collapsed into a single result.

Pathological complete response is a secondary endpoint. The registry reports a between-group difference of 21.13 percentage points (95% CI, 13.68–28.59) in the Stage IIB–IIIB wild-type population and 22.00 points (95% CI, 14.86–29.13) in the EGFR/ALK wild-type ITT population. The pathology result answers a narrower question: how often the surgical specimen met the registry’s definition of no viable tumor in the primary lesion and sampled lymph nodes. It does not mean every cancer cell in the body was eradicated, and it does not guarantee that recurrence will not occur. The outcome includes participants who never reached surgery as non-responders, so the denominator includes more than just the patients who underwent resection. Restricting it to operated patients could make a pre-operative strategy look more effective.

## Analysis populations and safety counts have different denominators

The denominator changes with the outcome being measured. The record lists 453 enrolled participants, while its participant-flow section says 451 took part: 225 were randomized to placebo plus chemotherapy and 226 to atezolizumab plus chemotherapy. Safety analyses included 224 and 226 people, while the primary efficacy measure used 201 and 196. Each outcome uses its own analysis population. The enrollment total is therefore not the denominator for every result.

In the safety population, serious adverse events were recorded for 72 of 224 participants in the control group and 105 of 226 in the atezolizumab group. During the all-cause mortality follow-up of up to 89.5 months, deaths from any cause were recorded for 92 of 224 and 81 of 226, respectively. The registry describes adverse events regardless of whether investigators considered them related to study treatment. Those death counts cannot establish that treatment caused or prevented deaths, and they are not an overall-survival comparison. All-cause event counts should not be presented as treatment-related rates. SAEs and other adverse events were collected for up to approximately 23.44 months; all-cause mortality was followed for up to 89.5 months.

The results were first posted on October 9, 2026, and the record lists the study as completed. That is a reporting event, not a regulatory approval or a new clinical guideline. The primary EFS comparison did not reach conventional statistical significance, while the ITT hazard-ratio confidence interval excluded 1 and has no posted p-value. Upper confidence limits for the atezolizumab medians could not be estimated. The results also apply to the named wild-type populations, not automatically to patients with EGFR or ALK alterations.

## The registry result needs the full statistical and clinical context

The posted results cover treatment before and after surgery. Pathological complete-response percentages were higher and reported EFS medians were longer in the atezolizumab group. The primary EFS comparison was not statistically significant at the conventional threshold. The registry reports an ITT hazard ratio but no p-value, so that estimate should be presented without inferring significance.

The next useful evidence is a peer-reviewed report that lays out the analysis plan, event counts, time-to-event estimates, safety attribution, and follow-up in enough detail to assess the comparison. Longer follow-up would also help clarify whether the difference in event-free survival corresponds to an overall-survival difference. The current registry evidence does not establish that the treatment extends life or replaces an individual oncology team’s judgment.

This article describes results entered in the public trial registry, not a peer-reviewed paper or a clinical guideline. Registry entries can be updated, and the posted numbers should be read with their stated populations and statistical limits.
