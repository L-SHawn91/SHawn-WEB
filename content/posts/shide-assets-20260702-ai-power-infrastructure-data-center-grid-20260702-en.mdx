## The short read

AI infrastructure does not stop at GPUs and HBM. More data-center capacity also requires electrical equipment, grid construction, cooling, transmission access, and reliable power supply.

## Why power comes after semiconductors

AI servers need dense racks, stable electricity, cooling, and grid connections. That is why the AI story now touches equipment makers, grid builders, utilities, nuclear power, and Korean transformer exporters.

## The opposite scenario belongs on the page

- AI data-center capex can slow.

- Grid permitting and interconnection can delay projects.

- Transformer demand can be strong while margins and delivery timelines remain uncertain.

- Nuclear and SMR expectations carry regulatory and execution risk.

- Higher rates can pressure long-duration infrastructure valuations.

- Strong price moves can reverse even when the long-term theme remains intact.

## Reader checklist

- Is this headline an actual order, a policy announcement, or an expectation?

- Is the data-center power theme connected to a specific company’s reported results?

- Are equipment makers and utilities being mixed into one overly broad story?

- Has price moved ahead of confirmed earnings?

- Are rates, permitting, regulation, and grid access included in the risk view?

## Bottom line

AI power infrastructure is a natural next chapter after semiconductors. But the theme is not one asset class. Equipment, grid construction, generation, utilities, and Korean transformer exporters each require different evidence.
