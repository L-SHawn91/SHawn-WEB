## Ten enrolled. Five evaluable. No CR or PR.

**Bottom line:** the newly posted ClinicalTrials.gov results do not support the strong response narrative previously attached to this trial. Ten patients entered the single-arm Phase I/II study, but only five were evaluable for efficacy. In those five, the registry reports no complete or partial responses across the entire disease burden and no complete or partial responses in non-injected tumors. Median progression-free survival could not be calculated. The study remains informative as an early safety and feasibility record, but it cannot establish systemic anti-tumor efficacy.

## 01 What was JX-594 designed to do?

JX-594, also called Pexa-Vec, is a thymidine-kinase-inactivated vaccinia virus engineered to express human GM-CSF. The trial administered it directly into melanoma lesions. This design aimed to combine local viral treatment with the possibility of broader anti-tumor effects, which is why the protocol separately measured injected tumors, non-injected tumors, and the entire disease burden. The posted results must be read endpoint by endpoint; those categories are not interchangeable.

## 02 Study design and the long data trail

NCT00429312 was an open-label, non-comparative, single-group Phase I/II study in unresectable Stage III or Stage IV malignant melanoma. Ten adults were enrolled at the Billings Clinic, the Cancer Center of the Carolinas, and UCLA. Mean age was 65.1 years (SD 11.2), and the cohort included five women and five men.

The planned regimen was 1 × 10 8  plaque-forming units of JX-594 by intratumoral injection once weekly for six treatments over six weeks. The initial tumor assessment was scheduled for Day 43. The study began in February 2007, reached primary completion in February 2008, and completed in December 2009. Results were first submitted in August 2015 but were not first posted until July 27, 2026. The registry does not explain this interval, so no reason should be inferred.

## 03 The denominator problem: 10 enrolled, 5 efficacy-evaluable

Participant flow shows that five patients completed the study and five did not. The flow module lists three incomplete efficacy evaluations, one participant withdrawal, and one physician decision. The outcome module adds detail: one patient lacked a CT scan, two assessments occurred more than 28 days outside the protocol window, and two patients withdrew before the Day 43 efficacy assessment.

This leaves an efficacy denominator of five—not seven and not ten. Half of the enrolled cohort was therefore absent from the efficacy-evaluable analysis. In a trial this small, that loss is not a footnote: it materially changes what the percentages and response labels can mean.

## 04 What the posted efficacy results actually show

**Injected tumors:** the primary outcome is titled “Number of Participants With Objective Response in Injected Tumor(s),” but the posted measurement unit is “Participants with Stable disease,” with a value of 5 among 5 evaluable participants. Because the registry does not post CR or PR categories for this endpoint, these five cases should be described as stable disease—not objective responses.

**Entire disease burden:** among five evaluable patients, the best overall RECIST response was CR 0, PR 0, SD 3, and PD 2.

**Non-injected tumors:** the same distribution was posted: CR 0, PR 0, SD 3, and PD 2 among five evaluable patients. These data do not demonstrate shrinkage of distant, untreated lesions. Stable disease in three patients may be a signal worth documenting, but a single-arm study cannot determine whether it resulted from JX-594.

**Progression-free survival:** the registry reports the median and 95% confidence interval as not calculable. Only five patients were efficacy-evaluable, and post-treatment tumor measurements were performed as standard care rather than collected systematically under the protocol. A PFS number should therefore not be supplied or reconstructed.

## 05 Safety requires two separate ledgers

The outcome table reports treatment-related adverse events in 7 of 10 participants and treatment-related serious adverse events in 0 of 10. The broader adverse-event module, which is not restricted to treatment-related events, records at least one non-serious event in all 10 participants. The most frequently listed were fatigue (7/10), injection-site erythema (4/10), anorexia (4/10), and anaemia (4/10).

The same broader module records serious events in two participants: acute gastrointestinal bleeding in one and spinal cord compression in one. Because the trial’s treatment-related SAE outcome is 0/10, these events must not be relabeled as treatment-related. Conversely, reporting only “two serious events” without the attribution distinction would make the treatment appear more toxic than the posted outcome supports.

## 06 What can be concluded?

**Supported:** the six-week intratumoral regimen was studied in ten patients; five were efficacy-evaluable; stable disease was recorded in injected tumors for those five; whole-disease and non-injected-lesion assessments showed three SD and two PD, with no CR or PR; and treatment-related adverse events occurred in seven patients.

**Not supported:** an objective-response claim based on an unexplained seven-patient denominator, partial responses in non-injected tumors, proof of a systemic immune effect, a calculable median PFS, or treatment-related attribution for the two serious events.

The trial’s main lesson is methodological as much as biological. In a ten-person study, the gap between enrollment and evaluability, the exact response category, and the distinction between treatment-related and all-cause adverse events determine the conclusion. Calling stable disease an objective response would convert a cautious feasibility signal into an efficacy claim the registry does not support.

## Evidence boundary

This article uses the protocol and results posted in the official ClinicalTrials.gov record for NCT00429312. It does not import response claims from secondary summaries or later JX-594 programs. The record provides aggregate results rather than patient-level timelines, and it contains a naming-versus-measurement inconsistency in the injected-tumor outcome; both limitations are stated rather than resolved by inference.
