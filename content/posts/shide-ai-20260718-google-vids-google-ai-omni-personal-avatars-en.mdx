## Google Vids Personal Avatars: AI Video Where You're the Star — No Camera Needed

KO · 한국어 / **EN · English** BILINGUAL PAIR

On July 16, 2026, Google announced two major updates to Google Vids: **Google AI Omni**, which lets you generate and edit videos with text prompts and image references, and **personal avatars**, which let you create a digital version of yourself from a single selfie and a short voice recording.

What changed with this update is that users can create a digital avatar that looks and sounds like them to deliver a message without setting up a camera.

## Two updates, one direction

### Google AI Omni: generate and edit with text prompts

Google Vids already offered text-to-video generation through Veo 3.1, rolled out to all users in February. Google AI Omni takes this further.

- Combine text and images as input. Describe a scene — “a person walking on a beach at sunset” — and add a reference photo or rough sketch. Omni blends both into a generated clip.

- Edit with text prompts. Type “swap the background to a forest,” “warm up the lighting,” or “add effects,” and Omni applies the change.

- Step-by-step editing. You can refine specific parts of a video without starting over from scratch.

_Text and image references become a generated clip that can be refined through step-by-step text-prompt edits._

### Personal avatars: one selfie, and you’re ready to film

The more striking update is personal avatars. Upload a selfie and a short voice recording, and Google Vids creates a digital avatar that looks and sounds like you. From there, type what you want to say — your avatar delivers the message. No camera setup, no re-recording.

Google has placed clear boundaries around this feature:

- Account-holder likeness only. You cannot create an avatar of someone else.

- Limited to certain regions and users 18 or older.

- Available to Google AI Pro, Ultra subscribers, and Google Workspace business customers.

## A separate update: custom AI avatars can perform directed actions

The June 17 Workspace update added text-prompt action direction to **custom AI avatars**. This was a separate feature update from the **personal avatars** announced on July 16.

Type instructions like “walk, talk, and use objects,” and the custom AI avatar follows them in the generated clip. Upload a brand logo or a location image, and the custom avatar moves within that space.

As described in the June update, AI avatars and voiceovers support 24 languages, including Korean, while custom avatars offer 30+ Google AI Audio voices. These details should not be assumed to apply identically to the personal-avatar feature announced in July.

## Transparency: SynthID watermarking

Every AI-generated clip includes an **invisible SynthID digital watermark.** It isn’t a visible label, but it can help verify that a clip was created with AI. Google describes it as a content-transparency measure.

## Why this matters

For users who found AI video tools difficult, prompt writing and repeated revisions could be a barrier.

This Google Vids update lowers the barrier in three concrete ways:

- Camera setup becomes optional. You can appear in videos without recording yourself on camera.

- Editing shifts toward text prompts. Type “brighten this,” “change the background,” or “add effects” instead of rebuilding the clip.

- Repeat production gets easier. Create your avatar once, then change the script to produce multiple videos.

This is practical for educators, brand marketers, customer communications, and individual creators alike.

## What to watch out for

- Avatars are not real people. They are AI-generated representations.

- AI responses can include mistakes. Google explicitly notes this.

- Personal avatars are not yet available worldwide. Regional restrictions apply.

- Free accounts are excluded. A paid Google AI or Workspace plan is required.

- The watermark is invisible. SynthID is a non-visible digital watermark — viewers cannot see it with the naked eye.

- A visible disclosure may still help. Because SynthID is invisible, public or sensitive videos may benefit from a separate on-screen or written AI-generation label.

- Selfies and voice recordings are personal data. Check your personal account or organization’s privacy and content policies before uploading them.
