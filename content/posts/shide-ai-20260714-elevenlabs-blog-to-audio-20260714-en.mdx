THE SHORT READ

With a free account and one article, you can turn a blog post into natural-sounding audio in minutes without recording anything — but decide where the audio will live before you start.

## Why turn a post into audio at all

The same article gets consumed in different ways. Some people listen on a commute, some search on YouTube, some only watch Shorts. Converting one post into audio gives you (1) a “listen” version of the article, (2) narration for a YouTube video, and (3) voice for Shorts/Reels — all from the same manuscript.

The point is reuse. You are not writing new material; you are converting an already-proven article into other channel formats.

## What you need: a free account is enough

All you need is an ElevenLabs account (free) and an article. The free tier gives 10,000 credits per month, and one character of text costs roughly one credit. At the 537-character sample size used in this article, that is enough for about eighteen short narrations a month.

No microphone, no booth, no editing software. A browser is enough.

## Step 1: paste the text

Open Text to Speech in the app and paste the part of your article you want to convert. Starting with a lead paragraph or a key summary, rather than a full article, makes credit management easier.

_Actual screen: after pasting 537 characters, the history panel shows the generation record and remaining credits (9,463)._

## Step 2: pick a voice and model

In the Settings tab, choose a voice and a model. For this sample we used the stock voice Roger (calm, low) with the Eleven Multilingual v2 model, unchanged. The multilingual model handles Korean and English in the same workflow, so if you run a KO/EN paired blog this combination is a sensible default.

![ElevenLabs voice and model settings](https://shawnaiintelligence.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/tut-02-voice-settings.png)

_The Settings tab: voice, model, and speed/stability sliders in one place._

## Step 3: generate and download

Press Generate speech and you are done. For 537 characters, generation took about 2 seconds, and the result was a 36-second MP3 (611KB), downloadable immediately. If you do not like the result, regenerate with the same text or switch voices.

## Limits and caveats (from actual use)

Three constraints we actually hit, recorded as-is. First, the free tier has IP-based abuse detection and can be temporarily blocked with an “Unusual activity detected” warning (it happened once during this session; a retry shortly after worked fine). Second, running search-engine ads on ElevenLabs brand keywords violates the affiliate terms and is grounds for account cancellation. Third, WordPress.com’s free plan does not allow MP3 uploads, so the realistic structure is hosting audio on an external channel such as YouTube and embedding it in the post.

## Bottom line

Turning text into audio is no longer an equipment or skill problem; it is a workflow decision. A free account, one article, and a few minutes produce a usable result.

The realistic order: start with short segments to learn the credit cost, decide where the audio will live (YouTube or similar) first, and only consider full-article conversion or a paid plan once you see actual audience response.
