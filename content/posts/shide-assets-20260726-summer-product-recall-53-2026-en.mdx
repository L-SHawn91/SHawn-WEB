Korea’s National Agency for Technology and Standards (KATS) tested **1,117 products** across 48 categories with high summer demand and issued recall orders for **53 products** that failed safety standards. This article breaks down what was recalled, why, and what consumers should check.

## 1) The Bottom Line: 53 Recalls Are Not a Market-Wide Defect Rate

According to the [KATS press release](https://www.motir.go.kr/kor/article/ATCL3f49a5a8c/172048/view) (2026-07-23), the inspection targeted summer-demand items including children’s clothing, umbrellas, parasols, and water toys. Of 1,117 tested, 53 failed — a **4.7% failure rate within this sample**.

This does not mean 4.7% of all summer products on the market are defective. The sample was concentrated on higher-risk categories and distribution channels.

> KATS conducts seasonal safety inspections every year, targeting products with elevated risk profiles. This summer inspection is part of that regular program.

**Lead (Pb)** is a heavy metal that can affect child development and may be detected in fabrics, coatings, and printed areas. **Phthalate plasticizers** are chemicals used to soften plastics, with reported endocrine-disrupting potential.

### Household and Electrical Products: 14 Recalls

Three water toys and two life jackets failed **buoyancy and load tests** — items directly tied to water safety.

Among seven electrical products, one plug/outlet failed the **temperature rise test** and one battery failed the **external short-circuit test**. Temperature rise failures are linked to overheating and fire risk.

## 3) What “Recall Order” Means Legally

Under Korea’s Framework Act on Product Safety and the Special Act on Safety of Children’s Products, the following measures apply to products that fail safety standards.

A recall order is a **legal obligation, not a recommendation**. Manufacturers or importers who fail to comply may face surcharges or criminal penalties.

## 5) How Consumers Can Check Right Now

To verify whether a purchased product is subject to recall:

- Visit the Product Safety Information Center (safetykorea.kr)

- Select “Recalled Products” or “Safety Inspection”

- Search by manufacturer, model number, or product category

- If matched, contact the manufacturer/importer for collection or exchange

**Consumer24** (consumer.go.kr) also provides recall notices and consumer dispute resolution procedures.

> If you already own a recalled product, do not panic. Contact the manufacturer or importer for collection, exchange, or refund procedures.

## 6) Overseas Direct-Purchase Summer Products: Separate Inspection Underway

## 9) Summer Product Safety Habits

In summer, sweat, water, and UV exposure accelerate product wear, and skin contact area increases. These habits help.

- Textiles : Wash new clothes before first wear; check KC mark and manufacturer info.

- Umbrellas/parasols : Inspect handle and rib finishes; for children’s items, verify hazardous substance compliance.

- Water toys : Check buoyancy rating (KC) and maximum load; choose life jackets sized by body weight.

- Electrical items (fans, mosquito repellers) : Verify KC certification and rated voltage; avoid power strip overload.

## 10) Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. I already used a recalled product. What if I have health concerns?**

A. File a dispute resolution request through Consumer24 (consumer.go.kr) or the Korea Consumer Agency (1372). Keep medical records for reference.

**Q. The manufacturer of my recalled product is unreachable. What now?**

A. Check collection compliance status on the Product Safety Information Center (safetykorea.kr). Report non-compliance to KATS.

**Q. Are overseas direct-purchase products included in this recall?**

A. The 53 recalls cover domestically distributed products. Overseas direct-purchase inspection results will be released separately in early August.

**Q. Does KC certification guarantee absolute safety?**

A. KC certification means minimum safety standards are met. Post-certification manufacturing changes or distribution damage are possible, so periodic product condition checks are advisable.
