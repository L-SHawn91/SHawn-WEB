From October 17, 2026, **halal certification becomes mandatory** for food, cosmetics, and pharmaceuticals imported into Indonesia. Korean exporters have approximately 84 days remaining. This article explains what becomes mandatory, what procedures Korean companies must follow, and what government support is available.

## 1) The Bottom Line: No Certification, No Customs Clearance After October 17

Indonesia’s Halal Product Assurance Agency (BPJPH) has been phasing in mandatory halal certification for domestically produced food since October 17, 2019. From **October 17, 2026**, this obligation extends to **imported food, cosmetics, and pharmaceuticals**.

Korea’s Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) [announced](https://www.mfds.go.kr/brd/m_99/view.do?seq=50060) (2026-06-08) that it would strengthen support for exporters ahead of this deadline, and on July 20 dispatched a **public-private joint support team** to Indonesia.

> Indonesia is the world’s largest Muslim-majority nation (approximately 240 million Muslims). Halal certification is both a religious requirement and an essential condition for market access.

## 3) Procedures Korean Companies Must Follow

- Raw material and manufacturing process halal compliance review — Confirm animal-derived ingredients (gelatin, glycerin, etc.), alcohol-based solvents, and cross-contamination risks

- Audit by BPJPH-recognized certification body (LPH) — On-site factory inspection in Indonesia or by an overseas body recognized by BPJPH

- BPJPH certificate issuance — Halal label usage rights granted upon audit completion

- Indonesian FDA (BPOM) import registration — Separate procedure from halal certification

## 8) Frequently Asked Questions

**Q. What happens if I export without halal certification after October 17?**

A. Customs clearance will be denied at Indonesian ports. Already-shipped cargo may be subject to return or disposal.

**Q. Do products with existing halal certification need re-certification?**

A. If within the certification validity period (typically 4 years), the certificate remains valid. However, additional documentation may be required due to the expanded scope for imported products.

**Q. Do vegan products also require halal certification?**

A. Yes. Halal certification audits the entire manufacturing and distribution process, not just ingredients. Even vegan ingredients require cross-contamination prevention systems.

**Q. How much does halal certification cost?**

A. Costs vary by product count, factory size, and certification body. Typical costs range from several million to tens of millions of KRW, with partial relief available through government support programs.

**Q. Do other Muslim-majority countries have the same requirements?**

A. Malaysia (JAKIM), UAE (ESMA), and Saudi Arabia (SFDA) also require halal certification. Certification bodies and procedures differ by country, so verification for each export destination is necessary.

## 9) Relationship Between Halal Certification and BPOM Registration

Halal certification (BPJPH) and import registration (BPOM) are **separate procedures**. Both must be completed for distribution in the Indonesian market.
