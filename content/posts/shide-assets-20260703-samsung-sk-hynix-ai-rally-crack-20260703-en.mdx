The AI memory story is still powerful. But stock prices do not move on good narratives alone. When Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix fell together, the market appeared to price in a combination of stretched expectations, foreign-flow pressure, global semiconductor weakness, and valuation sensitivity before waiting for the next round of earnings confirmation.

## The short version

The joint decline looks less like a single-company event and more like a **reset in expectations across the AI memory trade**. HBM demand may remain structurally important, but the market is now asking whether enough of that good news had already been priced in.

## Samsung and SK Hynix carry different burdens

Samsung is a broader company: HBM catch-up, commodity memory recovery, foundry, mobile, and non-memory all matter. SK Hynix is more directly associated with the AI memory premium and HBM leadership. That difference matters: the same selloff can reflect different expectations for each name.

## Takeaway

This decline does not mean the AI memory story is over. It suggests the market is separating a good long-term story from the expectations already embedded in price. The next stage depends less on slogans and more on HBM delivery, margins, inventory, foreign flows, and global semiconductor risk appetite.
